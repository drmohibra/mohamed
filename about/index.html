About

Mohamed Khair Ibraheem

Physician scientist at Mayo Clinic Arizona focused on critical care, clinical artificial intelligence, acute illness, clinical outcomes, and transplantation.

Mohamed Khair Ibraheem, MD, MS is a physician scientist whose work centers on clinically meaningful decision support in acute and critical illness. His research examines how prediction models and clinical artificial intelligence behave when outcome labels, testing patterns, missing information, and treatment decisions are influenced by clinical workflow.

His current work includes critical care, acute illness, sepsis, cardiovascular outcomes, clinical AI, kidney disease, transplantation, pulmonary medicine, and related high risk clinical populations.

Research perspective

A central theme of his work is whether a model is learning patient biology or patterns created by how the healthcare system observes patients. This includes outcome ascertainment, selective testing, interpretability, missing clinical information, model generalizability, and the transition from risk prediction toward individualized treatment decisions.

Clinical questions come before algorithms. The value of a model depends on the quality and meaning of the clinical evidence used to build it.

Clinical and academic background

His clinical foundation includes general surgery training and practice, followed by multidisciplinary clinical research spanning critical care, transplantation, nephrology, pulmonary medicine, cardiovascular outcomes, and clinical artificial intelligence. He earned an MS in General Surgery from Ain Shams University.
