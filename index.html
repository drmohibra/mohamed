Profile

Clinical questions before algorithms.

I am a physician-scientist with a clinical background in general surgery whose research sits at the intersection of acute and critical illness, clinical artificial intelligence, and decision science.

I study how outcome ascertainment, selective testing, missing clinical information, and treatment decisions shape what predictive models learn, and whether those models remain trustworthy when applied to real clinical care.

My work spans critical care, pulmonary medicine, kidney disease, transplantation, and cardiovascular outcomes. These are not separate research identities; they are clinical settings in which the same methodological problem repeatedly appears.

Are our models learning the patient's biology, or the way the healthcare system chose to observe the patient?
Research Program

What I study

My research is organized around the reliability of clinical evidence when observation, testing, treatment, and documentation are themselves selective.

Label Bias

Outcome ascertainment & selective testing

Clinical prediction models often learn labels created by testing decisions rather than biology alone. I am interested in how surveillance intensity, selective testing, and missing outcomes distort labels, apparent model performance, and downstream clinical conclusions.

Critical Care AI

Interpretable machine learning in acute illness

Work in sepsis and infection examines whether interpretability claims survive differences in population, workflow, prevalence, and deployment conditions, and whether explanation actually improves clinical usefulness.

Decision Science

From prediction to treatment decisions

Risk prediction asks who is likely to have an outcome. The more consequential question is who is likely to benefit from a specific intervention. This direction includes treatment-effect heterogeneity, causal inference, and individualized decision support.

Transplantation

Kidney, transplant & complex outcomes

Transplant and kidney populations provide clinically rich settings for studying surveillance, selection, longitudinal outcomes, treatment decisions, and the limitations of routinely collected clinical labels.

Clinical Domains

Cardiovascular, pulmonary & perioperative outcomes

Collaborative outcomes research in acute atrial fibrillation, pulmonary disease, surgery, and perioperative care extends the same focus on clinically meaningful prediction, treatment decisions, and generalizability.

Scientific Communication

Research in scientific exchange
